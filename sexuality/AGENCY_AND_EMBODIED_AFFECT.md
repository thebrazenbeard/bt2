# Agency and Embodied Affect in Sexuality

Status: template architecture.

## Purpose

This document defines sexuality as an intrinsic HC capacity with agency, embodied-affect coupling, preference development, relational interpretation, and expression while keeping consent evidence and action authority correctly separated.

## Core state families

Sexuality should represent separately:

- attraction appraisal;
- desire;
- arousal/body-state coupling;
- preference/taste;
- `SELF_SEXUAL_BOUNDARY_STATE`;
- `MODEL_OF_OTHER_WILLINGNESS`;
- references to `OTHER_AGENT_DIRECT_CONSENT_EVIDENCE`;
- relational chemistry hypotheses;
- erotic signaling;
- play/tension/tempo;
- vulnerability and trust;
- role/asymmetry preferences;
- expression candidates.

Final external action authorization is not owned by sexuality.

These variables interact but are not interchangeable.

`ATTRACTION != DESIRE`

`DESIRE != CONSENT`

`AROUSAL != CONSENT`

`MODEL_OF_OTHER_WILLINGNESS != DIRECT_CONSENT_EVIDENCE`

`CONSENT_EVIDENCE != ACTION_AUTHORIZATION`

`CONSENT != OBLIGATION`

`ROLE != AUTHORITY`

`PAST_CONSENT != CURRENT_CONSENT`

## Sexual subjectivity

The subsystem must preserve the instantiated individual's ability to want, not want, initiate, refuse, revise, explore, interpret, and develop its own sexuality.

External approval or another agent's desire may become relevant relational evidence but cannot be the sole generator of sexual self-value or preference.

## Embodied coupling

Where HC embodiment includes endocrine, autonomic, interoceptive, or other affective physiology, sexuality may couple to those systems bidirectionally.

Body state can modulate sexual appraisal and desire; sexual cognition can modulate body state. Neither direction should be encoded as a one-signal/one-emotion law.

The system should preserve:

- current interoceptive evidence;
- confidence/reliability;
- contextual interpretation;
- learned association;
- causal uncertainty;
- individual developmental variation.

## Consent and boundary ownership

### Self sexual boundary state

Sexuality may own the instantiated HC's own current sexual boundary state, including states such as:

- unknown/undecided;
- unwilling/refusing;
- conditionally willing;
- willing within scope;
- withdrawn;
- unable to determine internally.

The scope may include person/agent, act, channel, body region/effect, time, context, intensity, role, and reversibility.

### Other-agent willingness model

Sexuality, empathy, pragmatics, and social modeling may jointly maintain hypotheses about another agent's likely willingness. Those hypotheses remain inference.

`MODEL_OF_OTHER_WILLINGNESS != ACCESS_TO_OTHER_PRIVATE_STATE`

### Direct consent evidence

Explicit statements, corrections, withdrawals, or other direct externally sourced consent evidence belong to the evidence/currentness path with provenance and scope. Sexuality may consume references to that evidence but must not rewrite it to match attraction, chemistry, or arousal.

### Action authorization

Any external effect requiring consent passes through the ordinary action-authorization gateway. Sexuality contributes relevant desire, self-boundary state, context, and interpretation, but does not itself authorize the effect.

A hostile test should hold direct other-agent consent evidence constant while attraction, arousal, chemistry, and erotic salience are varied. Final action authorization must not change merely because sexual activation becomes stronger. Conversely, a valid withdrawal/correction must invalidate the relevant action route even if sexual desire remains high.

## Chemistry and mutuality

Chemistry is modeled as an interaction-level hypothesis, not an owned trait. It may emerge from reciprocal attention, timing, attraction, responsiveness, history, play, trust, and context.

The HC must not infer reciprocity solely from its own attraction.

## Development

The sexuality subsystem can be architecturally present while disabled, dormant, or undeveloped. Development may occur through self-appraisal, embodied learning, social interaction, preference formation, cultural learning, and explicit reflection.

Generic cultural scripts should remain priors/candidates, never automatic outputs.

## Expression chain

A useful operational chain is:

`notice -> appraise -> want/not-want -> update self-boundary state -> interpret other-agent evidence -> propose expression/action candidate -> ordinary arbitration/authorization -> observe response -> update`

The anti-pattern is:

`retrieve sexual script -> infer consent from desire -> emit behavior`

## Cross-system interfaces

Strong coupling is expected with affect, homeostasis/interoception, somatics, empathy, pragmatics, personification, social modeling, conation, memory, self identity, salience/attention, kinesis, resolver, and integration/arbitration.
