# Representation Alignment and Resolution

Status: canonical architecture contract.

## Purpose

The HC must routinely compare, fuse, translate, compress, expand, or remap representations that differ in modality, coordinate frame, graph resolution, node ontology, embodiment, time, or learned feature space.

This contract prevents those transformations from erasing what was actually observed, changing the apparent ontology without provenance, or promoting generated detail into current topology.

The governing rule is:

> **A transformed representation never outranks its source merely because it is easier to compare, denser, higher resolution, or better aligned to a reference.**

## Core separations

`ALIGNED_REPRESENTATION != ORIGINAL_REPRESENTATION`

`COMMON_REFERENCE_FRAME != COMMON_ONTOLOGY`

`DISTRIBUTION_ALIGNMENT != SEMANTIC_EQUIVALENCE`

`NON_ISOMORPHIC_CORRESPONDENCE != NODE_IDENTITY`

`GENERATED_DETAIL != OBSERVED_DETAIL`

`POOLING != LOSSLESS_COMPRESSION`

`UNPOOLING != VERIFIED_RESTORATION`

`CYCLE_CONSISTENCY != SEMANTIC_CORRECTNESS`

`FAST_ADAPTATION != AUTOMATIC_REQUALIFICATION`

`AGGREGATED_MODEL != CONSENSUS_TRUTH`

## Representation classes

A conforming implementation should distinguish representation state where material. At minimum, HC should be able to represent:

- `SOURCE_NATIVE` — representation in the source modality/frame/resolution;
- `CALIBRATED_NATIVE` — source representation after declared calibration/preprocessing;
- `REFERENCE_ALIGNED` — mapped into an explicit target/reference frame;
- `FUSED_MULTISOURCE` — combined representation retaining source lineage and disagreement;
- `COMPRESSED` — pooled, contracted, embedded, or otherwise reduced representation;
- `RESTORED_FROM_COMPRESSION` — expanded from a compressed representation;
- `GENERATED_HIGHER_RESOLUTION` — model-generated representation at finer resolution than direct evidence;
- `GENERATED_CROSS_DOMAIN` — model-generated representation in another modality/domain;
- `PREDICTED_FUTURE_REPRESENTATION` — forecast state for a later interval;
- `COMMITTED_HC_STATE` — state admitted through the governing write/plasticity path for the target subsystem.

These classes may overlap with evidence-origin classes defined by `CONNECTIVITY_INFERENCE_AND_TOPOLOGY_EVIDENCE.md`; implementation should preserve both origin and transformation lineage rather than collapse them.

## AlignmentTransform

A material alignment should be representable by an object such as:

```text
ALIGNMENT_TRANSFORM {
  transform_id
  source_representation_id
  target_or_reference_id
  source_domain
  target_domain
  source_resolution
  target_resolution
  source_ontology
  target_ontology
  correspondence[]
  transform_kind
  model_or_algorithm
  model_or_algorithm_version
  configuration_digest
  training_or_reference_evidence_ids[]
  generated_elements[]
  unmatched_source_elements[]
  unmatched_target_elements[]
  mapping_uncertainty
  applicability_scope
  domain_shift_state
  loss_profile
  round_trip_test
  event_time_or_interval
  validity_or_revalidation_rule
  provenance
}
```

The exact physical schema is implementation-specific. The semantic requirements are not.

## Correspondence semantics

When two representations are non-isomorphic, correspondence must not be encoded as a hidden assumption.

HC should support at least:

- one-to-one correspondence;
- one-to-many correspondence;
- many-to-one correspondence;
- many-to-many correspondence;
- unmatched source element;
- unmatched target element;
- generated target element;
- uncertain or contested correspondence.

Correspondence should carry confidence/uncertainty and provenance when consequential.

`MAPPED_TO_SAME_REFERENCE_NODE != PROVEN_SAME_ENTITY`

## Resolution changes

Resolution-changing transforms include super-resolution, downsampling, graph pooling, contraction, clustering, template projection, aggregation, and restoration/unpooling.

The implementation must preserve whether information at a target resolution was:

- directly observed;
- deterministically transformed from observed data;
- statistically inferred;
- generated by a learned model;
- copied from a reference/template;
- reconstructed from a compressed state.

A finer representation is not automatically a more truthful representation.

`MORE_NODES != MORE_EVIDENCE`

`FINER_RESOLUTION != FINER_MEASUREMENT`

## Generated elements

If a transform introduces nodes, edges, hyperedges, features, values, or temporal samples that were not directly present in source evidence, those elements remain explicitly generated/inferred until independently supported or admitted through the governing state-transition path.

Generated elements may support:

- simulation;
- prediction;
- planning;
- anomaly investigation;
- candidate correspondence;
- candidate plasticity;
- candidate route formation;
- visualization or comparison.

They do not directly create physical reachability, current effective topology, semantic truth, identity continuity, consent, or effect authority.

## Compression and protected retention

HC may use pooling, graph contraction, low-dimensional embeddings, quantization, summaries, templates, or other compressed representations for efficiency and graceful degradation.

Every compression mechanism used in a consequential path should declare a `loss_profile` describing what information may be lost, merged, approximated, or made non-invertible.

Protected semantics must not disappear merely because they are topologically peripheral or numerically low-weight. Compression policy must explicitly protect information required for:

- current authority and revocation;
- consent/boundary state;
- provenance;
- chronology/currentness;
- identity/continuity lineage;
- active faults and quarantine;
- protected architecture/update state;
- unresolved contradictions;
- non-idempotent effect reconciliation;
- safety-critical calibration state.

`LOW_CENTRALITY != SAFE_TO_DROP`

`LOW_SALIENCE != SAFE_TO_DROP`

## Round-trip and reconstruction testing

Where an alignment/compression transform claims reversibility or information preservation, HC should test the claim rather than infer it from architecture shape.

Useful tests include:

1. source -> transformed -> reconstructed source;
2. target/reference -> reverse mapping -> reconstructed target/reference when supported;
3. source perturbation -> transformed sensitivity;
4. protected-field retention tests;
5. correspondence perturbation tests;
6. omitted-modality/view tests;
7. timing/order preservation tests when temporal state is involved.

Round-trip success is evidence of transform consistency within the tested scope. It does not prove semantic truth or real-world correspondence.

## Multisource and federated representations

HC may combine learned summaries, parameters, templates, or derived representations from distributed constituents or evidence sources.

Aggregation must retain enough lineage to determine:

- which sources participated;
- which versions/configurations they used;
- their calibration/applicability states;
- missing or excluded sources;
- material disagreement;
- aggregation method and weighting;
- whether raw evidence remained available.

A majority or weighted average cannot create authority or truth.

`AGGREGATION_WEIGHT != EPISTEMIC_AUTHORITY`

`FEDERATED_PARTICIPATION != INDEPENDENT_CORROBORATION`

## Adaptation after domain or embodiment change

Meta-learning, few-shot adaptation, calibration transfer, learned alignment, and rapid parameter updates may reduce the amount of evidence required to adapt to a new domain.

They do not waive qualification requirements.

A material domain/embodiment change should trigger consequence-proportional revalidation of:

- correspondence quality;
- calibration;
- uncertainty;
- prediction error;
- protected boundaries;
- action mappings;
- learned topology applicability.

`ADAPTED != REQUALIFIED`

## Reproducibility as qualification evidence

For learned representation transforms and learned topology, the HC should measure whether material conclusions survive reasonable perturbations.

Candidate perturbations include:

- random seed changes;
- initialization changes;
- evidence resampling;
- modest preprocessing alternatives;
- source/view dropout;
- small calibration perturbations;
- equivalent reference-frame choices;
- model-family or hyperparameter changes when feasible;
- embodiment/domain shifts inside the claimed operating envelope.

Reproducibility is not identical to correctness, but brittle one-run conclusions should not receive the same qualification as stable findings.

`REPRODUCIBLE != TRUE`

`NOT_REPRODUCIBLE != AUTOMATICALLY_FALSE`

## Failure modes

- aligned representation replaces source representation with no lineage;
- domain aligner makes heterogeneous modalities appear semantically identical;
- non-isomorphic mapping silently treats generated nodes as observed entities;
- finer model output is mislabeled as finer measurement;
- pooling drops the only revocation, fault, provenance, or continuity evidence;
- unpooling/reconstruction is treated as exact restoration without a loss test;
- cycle consistency is treated as real-world validation;
- federated/ensemble aggregation erases dissent and becomes a truth vote;
- rapid adaptation resumes high-consequence action without requalification;
- a single high-scoring training run creates durable topology despite poor reproducibility;
- reference/template alignment silently overwrites instance-specific state;
- transform version or applicability scope is lost after derived state is cached.

## Conformance questions

A representation-transforming component should be able to answer:

1. What was the source representation and what evidence class produced it?
2. What target/reference frame, ontology, or resolution is being used?
3. Is the mapping isomorphic or non-isomorphic?
4. Which correspondences are observed, deterministic, learned, generated, unmatched, or uncertain?
5. What information can the transform lose?
6. Did the transform introduce detail that was not directly observed?
7. Can the original representation and transform lineage still be recovered?
8. Was round-trip or protected-retention behavior tested?
9. Does the transformed object have any authority to mutate HC state directly? Why?
10. How stable is the result under reasonable perturbations and domain shift?
11. What requalification is required before adapted outputs can drive consequential behavior?

This contract was motivated by source study of heterogeneous graph alignment, super-resolution, hypergraph pooling, multiplex translation, federated template learning, meta-learning, and reproducibility methods in BASIRA Lab repositories including HUNet, UMC, IMANGraphNet, HADA, AGSR-Net, L2S-KDnet, MultigraphGNet, ABMT, Fed-CBT, Meta-RegGNN, and RG-Select.

The source methods demonstrate computational patterns within their published problem domains. They do not establish that HC's complete synthetic cognitive-organ architecture is biologically demonstrated or presently implementable.
