# HC-2 Noöplex-Q

Status: canonical generation architecture.

## Formal designation

**HC-2 Noöplex-Q — Quantum-Augmented Synthetic Hyperconnective Neuroglial Encephalon**

## Generation role

HC-2 inherits the complete HC-1 cognitive organ and adds specialized quantum, photonic, or closely related accelerator substrates where they provide demonstrated utility.

The accelerator layer is not the architectural locus of organism identity, memory authority, values, executive cognition, or action authority. It is an HC-owned specialized compute substrate participating through typed internal interfaces.

`HC2 = HC1_COMPLETE + SPECIALIZED_ACCELERATION`

## HC ownership and physical placement

HC-2 follows the distributed physical-organ rule. A QPU or photonic processor may be cranial, cervical, torso-mounted, or otherwise physically distributed and still be part of the HC if it satisfies the HC membership contract:

- HC owns its function and state;
- it participates as internal cognitive substrate or fabric;
- its lifecycle/faults are governed as HC state;
- it is included in complete-organ transfer, replacement, or explicit HC repair.

Physical distance from the skull does not make it a body peripheral.

A true external QPU is allowed only as an optional bounded computational peripheral. If removing it uniquely removes an essential HC-2 cognitive function, it was misclassified and belongs inside the HC organ boundary.

## Accelerator classes

Candidate implementation families include:

- photonic interference/linear-algebra accelerators;
- photonic quantum processors;
- spin/defect-center systems;
- superconducting QPUs;
- neutral-atom or trapped-ion systems;
- later qualified quantum or hybrid accelerators.

The architecture does not privilege one technology absent an implementation-specific evidence case.

## Classical-quantum bridge

Neural or neuromorphic activity does not directly encode arbitrary quantum gate programs.

A bounded HC-internal bridge performs, as applicable:

1. workload classification;
2. input encoding/compilation;
3. hardware control;
4. execution;
5. measurement;
6. classical post-processing;
7. return of typed results with uncertainty/provenance.

The receiving HC coalition treats accelerator output as evidence/service result rather than automatic belief or authority.

`QPU_RESULT != HC_DECISION`

`ACCELERATION != AUTHORITY`

## Plausible workloads

Quantum offload is appropriate only where a concrete algorithm/hardware combination warrants it. Candidate domains include:

- quantum-system simulation;
- selected chemistry/materials problems;
- some sampling tasks;
- cryptographic algorithms where hardware and fault tolerance permit;
- amplitude-estimation-style workloads;
- optimization only where demonstrated advantage exists.

The architecture explicitly rejects generic claims of quantum speedup for ordinary perception, language, memory, emotion, or social cognition.

## Photonic support

Photonic subsystems may provide high-bandwidth routing, low-latency transforms, optical multiplexing, or quantum photonics where appropriate.

Classical photonic analog acceleration and quantum computation must remain distinct.

## Quantum sensing

Defect-center and related quantum sensors may support precision measurement of magnetic field, temperature, strain, timing, or chemistry within their actual qualified envelopes.

Whole-brain neuron-by-neuron magnetic readout is not assumed without explicit speculative designation.

## Thermal architecture

HC-2 may contain multiple thermal domains.

The neural/biohybrid substrate has first-priority thermal protection. Accelerators must throttle, isolate, or drop out before forcing essential cognitive substrate outside its qualified operating envelope.

A technology requiring deep cryogenics may still be HC-internal if its cryogenic module is physically distributed but owned by and transferred with the HC organ. The previous notion that such hardware must be an external cold-dock is not canonical.

Detailed support and distributed-organ rules are defined in `docs/engineering/POWER_THERMAL_AND_DISTRIBUTED_ORGAN.md`.

## Failure and graceful degradation

Generation-specific acceleration should fail visibly and locally when possible.

A QPU or photonic-accelerator fault should expose:

- affected workload classes;
- whether fallback exists;
- confidence in returned results;
- thermal/resource effects;
- requalification requirements;
- whether the fault changes HC-2 conformance.

Where possible, HC-2 should operate in an HC-1-like capability envelope without losing identity continuity, memory, authority, or essential cognition.

This does not change generation identity: a damaged or throttled HC-2 remains HC-2 with degraded health/capability state.

`DEGRADED_HC2 != HC1`

## Programming and policy

HC-2 may expose engineering controls for accelerator scheduling, encoding, policy, thermal QoS, diagnostics, calibration, and result validation.

These controls do not permit accelerator infrastructure to bypass normal cognitive adjudication, action authority, memory admission, or identity-continuity rules.

## Evidence boundary

Quantum processors, integrated photonics, quantum sensors, neuromorphic systems, and hybrid control stacks are component research/engineering domains informing HC-2. Their integration into a complete HC-series synthetic cognitive organ at this scale remains speculative engineering.

No claim of generic quantum cognition or universal quantum advantage is made. Scientific claims are governed by `docs/science/EVIDENCE_BOUNDARIES.md` and require appropriate source support before receiving `DOCUMENTED` status.
